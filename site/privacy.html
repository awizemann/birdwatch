Privacy

Last updated August 14, 2026

Birdwatch is a local diagnostic tool. It reads the state of the iCloud sync daemons already running on your Mac and shows it to you — that's the entire product. There is nothing to collect, because there's nowhere for data to go.

Everything stays on your Mac

Birdwatch runs entirely on your machine. It doesn't have a server, and it doesn't send anything anywhere — not to us, not to anyone else.

No analytics, no telemetry

Birdwatch doesn't track usage, crashes, or behavior. There's no analytics SDK in the app.

No accounts

You don't sign in to Birdwatch, and it doesn't create or need an account. It uses whatever iCloud account is already signed in to your Mac, the same way Finder does.

No network calls of its own

Birdwatch doesn't make its own network requests. It reads local system state — daemon status, log output, and sync metadata already present on disk and in memory — through system APIs such as brctl, NSMetadataQuery, and log stream. Any network activity you see reflected in the app (like estimated bandwidth) belongs to macOS's own iCloud daemons, not to Birdwatch.

What it reads, specifically

  • Sync status and progress from the CloudDocs daemon (bird)
  • Sync status and item counts from the CloudKit daemon (cloudd)
  • Domain status from File Provider extensions (fileproviderd)
  • System log output for these daemons, streamed locally
  • CPU and memory usage of these daemons, sampled locally

None of this leaves your Mac. Full Disk Access is required to read it, which is why Birdwatch asks for that permission on first launch.
